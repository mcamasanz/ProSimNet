Modeling the propagation of drying and decomposition
fronts in wood
Antonio Galgano, Colomba Di Blasi∗
Dipartimento di Ingegneria Chimica, Universitá degli Studi di Napoli “Federico II, ” P . le V . Tecchio, 80125 Naples, Italy

Available online 19 August 2004
Abstract
Decomposition of moist wood is modeled using the shrinking unreacted-core approximation for a ﬁnite rate
of reaction and the assumption of a thermally controlled evaporation of moisture across an inﬁnitely thin front at
constant temperature. The one-dimensional, quasi-steady (along the char layer) equations also take into account
convective, conductive, and radiative heat transfer and different physical properties for char, dry wood, and moist
wood. The use of realistic values for all the input parameters results in acceptable agreement between predicted
and measured weight losses for 0.04-m-thick beech wood particles (external radiative heat ﬂuxes between 40 and
80 kW/m
2 and initial moisture contents between 0 and 47 wt% on a dry basis). A parametric analysis indicates
that, apart from a linear dependence on the moisture content, the characteristic times are especially affected by wa-
ter evaporation in the case of thick samples. It has also been found that assuming a coincident front for both wood
decomposition and moisture evaporation, often done in gasiﬁcation/combustion models, could be applied with
sufﬁcient accuracy only for thick samples with high moisture contents and subjected to severe thermal heating.

1. Introduction

cellulosic materials under extreme temperature con-
ditions has long been recognized, as they play a large
part in building ﬁres. Another application, of increas-
ing industrial interest, is related to thermochemical
conversion (pyrolysis, gasiﬁcation, combustion) to re-
cover liquid and gaseous fuels and energy. These ap-
plications have motivated the development of detailed
pyrolysis models dealing with the description of all
the main transport phenomena and relevant chemistry

The most recent study [23], focused on thermo-
chemical conversion processes, presents a compre-
hensive shrinking unreacted-core model for the pyrol-
ysis of a single wood particle, taking into account un-
steadiness and spatial temperature gradients for both
the virgin and the charred solid, ﬁnite-rate kinetics,
convective transport, and realistic property values and
kinetic constants. The model has been experimentally
validated and an extensive analysis of sensitivity to
model assumptions and parameter values has been
made. However, despite the scarce use of predried fu-
els in practical applications and the recognized effects
of moisture evaporation on charring rate, product
yields, and gas-phase combustion of volatile products,
drying processes are completely neglected, as gener-
ally done in previous shrinking unreacted-core mod-
els of wood pyrolysis. An exception is represented by
the work carried out by Palchonok et al. [24],w h i c h ,
however, is more simpliﬁed owing to the assumptions
of inﬁnite-rate kinetics for the pyrolysis reaction, ab-
sence of convective transport, and steady state formu-
lation of the enthalpy equations for both the char and
the dried wood layers.
Moisture evaporation has been shown to increase
the ignition time, the total ignition energy, and the
minimum intensity for both spontaneous and piloted
ignition [25]. The composition of the mixture of
volatile pyrolysis products is also altered [26] with its
energetic content, which, during combustion, also af-
fects the burning rate of the solid. The increase in the
initial moisture content has been reported to promote

4. Conclusions
The shrinking unreacted-core model of wood py-
rolysis, previously developed and validated [23],h a s
been extended to include the effects of moisture evap-
oration. This process, which is of great importance
in practical applications and is known to affect the
process variables dramatically, was not taken into ac-
count in previous simpliﬁed models of wood pyrol-
ysis. In this respect, the effort done in this study is
a signiﬁcant advance in the development of reliable
single-particle models to be coupled with CFD mod-
els for reactor simulation.
Moisture evaporation has been assumed to occur
at an inﬁnitely thin evaporation front at a known tem-
perature coincident with the normal boiling point of
water. The model correctly predicts the qualitative
trends observed in the experiments of single particles
exposed to thermal radiation. Furthermore, a compar-
ison with measured weight loss characteristics, ob-
tained for a wide range of initial moisture contents
and external heat ﬂuxes, indicate that simulation re-
sults are also acceptable from the quantitative point
of view.
As expected the drying and conversion times
present an almost linear dependence on the initial
moisture content. Moreover, though the effects of this
parameter do not appear to depend on the external
heating conditions, they become quantitatively more
important for successively thicker particles.
The initial moisture content also highly affects
other process variables (reactivity, pyrolysis tempera-
ture, maximum devolatilization rate), but percent vari-
ations are practically independent of both particle size
and external heat ﬂux.
The model has also been used to assess the ac-
curacy of the assumption, made in previous stud-
ies of wood gasiﬁcation/combustion, concerning the
existence of a single front where moisture evapora-
tion and wood decomposition occur simultaneously.
It has been found that this treatment could be applied,
though with some approximation, only for very thick
and moist samples under high level heat ﬂuxes.
